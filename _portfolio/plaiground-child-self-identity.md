**Pilot workshop with the University of Oulu, Kotilo Children's Cultural Centre, and Cultural Inquiry (Switzerland) · May 2026**

## Overview
A pilot study exploring how children aged 7–12 represent their present and future self-image, comparing a fully analog self-portrait activity against AI-assisted and AI-only versions of the same creative task.

## Objectives
- Examine how children's creative process and sense of authorship shift with and without AI involvement
- Assess whether AI tools can meaningfully represent a child's self-image, or distort it
- Identify the learning and creativity implications of AI-constrained versus open creative tasks

## Methodology
- Three sequential scenarios: hand-drawn self-portrait (no AI) → AI-animated version of the drawing → AI-only image generation via an educational AI tool
- Observational templates tracking participation across each stage
- Conducted with 8 children (ages 9–10) and 4 accompanying adults, under university and cultural centre ethics protocols, including parental consent

## My Contribution
Involved in workshop planning, pre-session discussions, and data collection.

## Outcomes
Early findings suggest the analog, hands-on self-portrait produced the strongest connection to children's genuine self-image, while AI-mediated versions sometimes disrupted this connection — informing a proposed "Pl-AI" framework for more child-centered AI design.
